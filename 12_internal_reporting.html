Before a trade reaches a regulator, it reaches the firm’s own reports: the daily P&L, the position, the confirmation, the invoice, the ledger. Same book, different readers — and each one checks it from a different side.

with Elizabeth Cecilia. What a trade is worth, what was actually paid, and where a wrong booking gets caught.

01

Front, middle, back

The front office agrees trades and captures them in the ETRM. The middle office turns the book into numbers management runs on: daily P&L, positions, exposure against limits. The back office confirms each trade with the counterparty, settles it, invoices or pays, and reconciles the cash into the general ledger.

All of them read the same golden source that feeds REMIT and EMIR. A trade booked wrong in the ETRM is wrong in the P&L, the invoice and the regulatory report at once — which is why the internal reports are often where a reporting error is first noticed.

03

The explain has to add up

A daily P&L explain breaks the change in value into causes: the curve moved, new trades were booked, old trades were amended. The bars have to add up to the difference between yesterday’s and today’s value. Whatever doesn’t is unexplained P&L, and unexplained P&L is a break: a missing price, a wrong booking, a trade that changed without anyone saying so.

Notice the amendment bar. Fixing a price from €61 to €60 is worth €7,200 on paper and changes nothing in the market. It is also exactly the kind of change that has to flow on to the regulatory reports, where the same trade went out at the wrong price. Under EMIR, that is a correction to report.

04

Follow a mistake front to back

Settlement turns the book into cash. Trades are confirmed with the counterparty, delivered, settled against invoices or settlement statements — including imbalance settlement with the TSO for physical power — paid, and posted to the general ledger. Each step has its own check, and each check sees a different kind of error first.

05

The rule, in one breath

P&L says what the book is worth, settlement says what was paid, and both check the same book the regulators see.

Marked to the curve, profit is unrealised until delivery and settlement. The P&L explain has to add up; what doesn’t is a break. Confirmations catch booking errors first — and every fix in the book is a fix owed to the regulatory reports.
